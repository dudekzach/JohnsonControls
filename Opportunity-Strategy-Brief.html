1. Executive Readout — What Changed

The original brief was written from a document. Four sessions of actual customer contact have moved almost every conclusion.

The revised core read

The technical risk in this deal has largely evaporated. The commercial risk has not moved at all. Four sessions in, the POC has been simplified, de-risked, and scoped down to something Genesys will almost certainly deliver on time. In the same four sessions, not one word has been said about budget, contract position, decision process, or an economic buyer. We are executing well on an evaluation whose commercial shape we still cannot describe.

The five things that matter now

  1. The POC will no longer answer the architecture question. There is no live API integration and no NICE integration in scope — Genesys hosts the data and simulates the API. That was the right call for the schedule, but it means the coexistence-versus-front-door question that determines the commercial ceiling of this deal is not being tested. It will be answered in a commercial conversation nobody has scheduled. SA Interpretation
  2. The automated happy path has narrowed to a point where POC containment will look low — and containment is a stated scoring criterion. This is the most consequential finding in this revision and it is treated in full in §4.
  3. Section 15 is now the win vehicle, and it is yours. Todd and Zach are dividing the Vendor Response Checklist responses into a separate tracker outside the FRS. Every differentiation theme in the original brief — improvements, reporting, outbound, chat/SMS, production readiness, commercial estimate — is a row in that spreadsheet. It matters more to the outcome than any demo. Document Fact (session 09/02)
  4. Three capabilities JCI explicitly asked Genesys to make a recommendation on have gone quiet. FAQ scope, chat/SMS use case, and dialer scenarios were all live asks on 08/24. Web messaging picked up an owner. FAQ and dialer have not been mentioned in three sessions since. See §8.
  5. The pre-POC demo window is gone. Build finishes next week and testing follows immediately. The four-scene demo plan was sequenced around a pre-POC slot that no longer exists. Revised sequencing in §10.

Revised posture

Pursue, with the emphasis moved off delivery and onto qualification. The delivery team is in good shape and JCI is engaged, cooperative, and unusually clear about what it wants. What we do not have, after four sessions and two document packages, is any evidence that this is a funded initiative with a buyer. That gap is now the deal's dominant risk, and it is not a technical one.

4. The Containment Paradox

The most important new finding in this revision. Read this section before the readout is scheduled.

The facts

Mario stated that roughly 70% of the business unit's calls are System Test-On requests, and that the existing system achieves roughly 20% containment after about two years. He also stated the evaluation will assess execution speed, customer journey, intuitiveness, containment versus agent routing, and success rates for qualifying alarms. Document Fact (sessions 08/26 and 09/01)

Now count what the agreed POC design routes to an agent:

ConditionOutcome
System Test-Off requestAgent
Partial test requestAgent (after identifying the system)
Combo system (fire + burglary on one CS number)Agent
HVAC-related alarmAgent
Multiple primary and secondary panels (2Pri/2Sec/1NonMon)Agent
PIC invalid, unlisted, unrecognized, or lacking test authorizationAgent
Requested duration over the state maximumAgent
Start time more than 5 minutes in the pastAgent
End time already passedNot allowed
System currently in alarmAgent
Identification fails after the full fallback ladderAgent
Contact search needed (API unavailable)Out of scope

What automates end to end is a narrow band: a standalone fire or burglar system, single primary with at most a secondary, full test, valid PIC, duration inside the state maximum, start time now or future within thirty days.

The risk

JCI intends to execute all ~80 test scenarios, and a large share of those scenarios are designed to produce an agent transfer. Business partners outside the core working group may be running them. If nobody frames this in advance, a scenario set that is largely composed of correct refusals will read as a system that transfers constantly — measured against a criterion explicitly named as containment versus agent routing, and against an incumbent baseline of 20%.

The sleeper variable

Nobody has established what share of JCI's monitored base is combo. Combo systems share one central station number across fire and burglary functions covering different building areas — which is a very common configuration in commercial fire and security. Combo transfers to an agent for the MVP. If combo is a large fraction of the base, the POC's demonstrated containment ceiling is a scope artifact with no relationship to what the platform could achieve. If combo is a small fraction, this is a non-issue. We do not know which, and it is a single question to ask. SA Interpretation Validate

What to do about it, in order

  1. Ask Darrell for the combo and HVAC share of the monitored base before testing starts. One question, potentially decisive to how the readout is interpreted.
  2. Separate the two containment numbers explicitly, in writing, before testing. Containment within the automated scope, and containment across all traffic. The first is what the POC measures. The second is what production would deliver once combo, partial, and Test-Off are automated. Put both in the Section 15 reporting and testing rows.
  3. Reframe agent transfers as designed outcomes in the test guidance. A correct refusal on an in-alarm system is a safety behavior, not a containment failure. This belongs in the knowledge-transfer material Mario asked for, which is going to personas outside the working group.
  4. Use the exclusions as roadmap, not apology. Combo, partial, and Test-Off are the three obvious phase-two automations, each already scoped and understood. That is a production conversation handed to us on a plate — and it is the natural bridge to the commercial discussion nobody has started.

5. The Architecture Question Is Now Unanswered, Not Answered

The single most important strategic consequence of the simplified POC.

The original brief identified the architecture decision — Genesys as the voice front door versus Genesys coexisting behind NICE — as the factor that sets the commercial ceiling of this deal. That judgment stands. What has changed is that the POC has been designed in a way that tests neither option.

There is no NICE integration in scope. There is no SIP dependency, no virtual number provisioning, no Studio script import, no JSON post-back. Nothing in four sessions references the contact center platform at all. The POC is a self-contained Genesys build against hosted data tables. Document Fact

The good news

Every dependency on a competitor's platform and every dependency on JCI's API team has been removed from the critical path. The delivery risk that dominated the original brief is gone. And a self-contained build implicitly demonstrates the full-platform option rather than the bolt-on one — routing, queues, wrap codes, scripts, reporting, and now web messaging are all being built in Genesys.

The consequence

A successful POC proves Genesys can build the journey. It does not prove Genesys can be dropped in front of NICE, and it does not prove JCI should replace NICE. Whichever way JCI leans commercially, the POC gives them no evidence either way — which means the decision gets made on price, incumbency, and relationship. Those are the three grounds on which an incumbent normally wins.

Recommendation: the production-readiness and integration rows of the Section 15 response are where this gets addressed. Answer both architectures explicitly and honestly — what a NICE-fronted deployment would require, what a Genesys-owned voice path would require, and what each costs JCI in reporting fidelity, agent experience, and change management. Do not argue for one. Lay out the trade and let the analysis do the work. That document becomes the artifact the commercial conversation runs on. SA Interpretation

6. Positioning Themes — Revised

What survived contact with the customer, what got stronger, and what has to be retired.

Stronger than assessed — single-platform breadth

JCI asked Genesys to author the chat/SMS use case and to propose dialer scenarios. Miguel deliberately withheld a use case so Genesys could show its own approach. This is no longer a differentiation we assert; it is a deliverable the customer requested and will compare across vendors. It is also the requirement the incumbent conversational vendor structurally cannot answer.

Stronger than assessed — operating model and administrative ownership

The knowledge transfer Mario specified covers platform terminology and navigation for IT users, customer-facing interaction handling, and supervisor and manager views for monitoring agent performance. That is not bot-evaluation scope. That is a customer evaluating whether they could run the platform. Treat the knowledge-transfer sessions as a selling moment, not a handover chore.

Weaker than assessed — unified analytics

Mario said normal call data is sufficient and that advanced journey-management analysis is not required for this POC. The unified funnel argument was the strongest theme in the original brief and it now has no scoring weight. Keep it, but move it from a POC differentiator to a production and roadmap argument, and lead the reporting conversation with the outcome logging JCI actually asked for: successful placement, error, hang-up, ineligible account, and caller electing an agent.

Retire — agentic multi-value capture as a headline

Still true and still valuable, but the POC's data capture is now short: identify, disambiguate by address, PIC, full-test confirmation, start and end date/time. The dramatic turn-count contrast against the old ten-step ladder no longer exists to demonstrate, because the ladder is not being rebuilt. Rich also noted Genesys would assess virtual agents, AI-powered slots, and agentic options rather than defaulting to traditional NLU — so this is already inside the build rather than a differentiator we bring separately.

New — safety-appropriate design judgment

The design decisions of the last two sessions are a coherent story: refuse to automate combo and HVAC until standalone flows are validated; transfer on an active alarm; secure and do not transcribe the PIC; a male, professional, reassuring voice suited to life-safety rather than a cheerful assistant. Presented as a deliberate set rather than as individual scope exclusions, this reads as vendor judgment in a life-safety context. That is a credibility asset with operations leadership and it costs nothing to assemble.
