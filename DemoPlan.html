1. DSP Foundation

Everything downstream traces to this. Built from JCI's own statements across four working sessions and both data workbooks.

Customer objectives and the metrics that matter

The volume anchor: roughly 70% of the business unit's call volume is System Test-On. That single number is why this narrow journey carries a real business case. to-verify — total annual call volume, AHT, and fully loaded cost per contact are all still unknown, so absolute savings cannot yet be stated.

Top challenges

  • Containment has plateaued at roughly a fifth of eligible traffic after two years. The problem is not that automation was attempted; it is that it stopped improving.
  • The caller journey is slot-by-slot. The current design asks for central station number, then account phone, then street number, then postal code, then a spelled PIC, then start date, then start time, then end date, then end time, then name — each its own turn with its own error ladder.
  • Callers are asked to state a sensitive identifier out loud because address-based disambiguation was not available in the current design.
  • Failure and refusal are handled as errors rather than as outcomes. A caller who cannot place a test lands on an agent with no context.
  • Change is slow and externally owned, which is why the intent set and FAQ inventory have not grown.
  • Operational consequence sits outside the contact center. A system not placed on test generates alarm activity and downstream dispatch cost. to-verify — JCI has not supplied a false-dispatch volume or cost.

Differentiators, tied to this situation

  • One platform across the whole SOW. Self-service, routing, agent desktop, outbound, and digital on one interaction record. The incumbent conversational vendor cannot answer the outbound section of JCI's own document.
  • JCI owns the change. Prompts, intents, knowledge, and rules administered by JCI in Genesys Cloud CX rather than through a vendor ticket.
  • Agent-side value they did not ask for. Context, transcript, summary, and next-step guidance on the transfers the design deliberately creates.
  • Deterministic rules, conversational surface. Eligibility decisions execute as explicit logic in Genesys Cloud Architect, not as model judgement — which is the right answer in a life-safety function.
  • Design judgement in a safety context. Refusing to automate combo and HVAC until standalone flows validate; transferring on an active alarm; securing the PIC; a professional rather than cheerful voice. Presented as a set, this is a credibility asset.

Scene A — Outbound: Prevent the Call

15 minutes · Operations leadership, the outbound owner, Ulysses and Miguel · Deliver in the demo/sales-team session

Objective
Prevent the operational failure that inbound self-service exists to absorb
Metric
Expiring test windows extended without an inbound call; inbound Test-On deflection; unnecessary alarm activity
Capability
Genesys Cloud Outbound — agentless notification, with preview and predictive modes shown for completeness
Outcome
Outbound stops being a capability checkbox and becomes the second half of the same business case

Why this scene goes first: JCI took the dialer question away on 24 August to form an internal view and has not come back. A recommendation lands far better before their opinion sets than after. It is also the one requirement in JCI's own SOW that the incumbent conversational vendor cannot answer at all.

Talk track

  1. Open on the problem, not the product
    Say

    "Before I show you anything, I want to check I understand something. A test window expires at a site and nobody extends it. The technician is still working. What actually happens next in your world?"

    [Let them answer. They will describe the alarm activity and the dispatch consequence better than you can.]

    "Right. So everything we've built over the last four weeks is about handling that call well. This next part is about not needing the call."

    ShowNothing yet. Screen stays on the problem.
  2. Controls before dialing
    Say

    "I'm going to start with the boring part on purpose, because in your business it's the part that matters. Before anything gets dialled, this decides who is allowed to be contacted."

    ShowContact list, list filters, contactable time sets with local-time-zone mapping, DNC list assignment, attempt controls per contact and per number.
    Say

    "Contactable time sets mean you're calling inside the contact's local window, not yours. Attempt controls cap how often the same contact and the same number can be tried. A do-not-contact match removes the record from selection entirely. And a wrap-up code can mark a number — or the whole contact — uncallable from then on."

    "These are controls, not compliance. Your compliance function decides which of them have to be switched on and how. I'm not going to tell you that a configuration satisfies a regulation — I don't know your consent posture or your contact population. I'd rather have that conversation with the person who owns it."

  3. The recommended use case, live
    Say

    "Here's what I'd actually recommend you do first. An agentless campaign — no agent reserved, no queue consumed. It calls the authorised contact before the window closes and offers to continue or extend."

    ShowAgentless campaign configuration, Call Analysis Responses, the Architect outbound flow handling a live answer versus an answering machine. Run it. Show the result recorded against the contact.
    Say

    "That call cost you no agent time. And if the contact says yes, the inbound call you would otherwise have handled never happens."

  4. Answer the dialer question properly, then move on
    Say

    "You asked us to come back with a view on dialer scope, so let me be complete rather than clever about it."

    ShowBriefly: preview mode presenting record and script before the call; predictive pacing configuration including compliance abandon rate; manual dial from Agent Workspace on behalf of a queue.
    Say

    "Preview is where an operator sees the record and the script and decides. Predictive is for volume — it paces from your actual agent behaviour and you control the compliance abandon rate. And an operator can place a call on behalf of a queue so the statistics and the after-call work stay with the right business queue rather than disappearing into an individual's numbers."

    "My recommendation is you start with the agentless notification. It needs no agent capacity, it's the lowest-risk thing on this list, and it targets the same cost as the journey we just built. The other two are there when you want them."

  5. Land it on the reporting point
    ShowCampaign Performance alongside the inbound flow outcome reporting. Same analytics framework, same interaction attributes.
    Say

    "Last thing. The campaign results and the inbound Test-On results are in the same reporting, on the same platform, against the same contacts. So you can actually answer the question 'did the notification reduce the calls' — instead of comparing two vendors' exports and hoping."

The pivot — say this before you leave the scene

Say

"One observation, and then I'll stop. Your SOW asks a single vendor about self-service, agent handling, outbound dialling and digital channels. We've now shown you all four in the same tenant. Your current conversational platform can answer one of them. That's not a criticism of them — it's a question about how many platforms this needs to be."

Discovery to extract here

  • How many test windows expire without extension in a month, and what does the resulting activity cost? (Targets M)
  • Who owns outbound today, and what platform is it on? (Targets E and Competition)
  • If you wanted the notification campaign in production, whose budget is that? (Targets E — ask it plainly, it is a natural question here)

Scene B — Copilot and the Supervisor View: Handle the Refusal Well

20 minutes · Central station operations, supervisors, Miguel and Ulysses · Deliver inside the technical knowledge-transfer session

Objective
Resolve the calls self-service correctly refuses, without the caller starting over
Metric
AHT on transferred Test-On calls; caller re-identification rate; containment visibility
Capability
Participant data and screen pop, transcript and auto-summary, Genesys Agent Copilot, Performance views and Flow Outcome reporting
Outcome
An exception transfer becomes a designed handoff, and the supervisor can see the whole journey for the first time
Register matters here

This sits inside a knowledge-transfer session Mario asked for. Deliver it as "here is how you would operate this," not "here is why we are better." The capability speaks; you do not have to.

Talk track — the operator half

  1. Frame the refusal as correct
    Say

    "We deliberately built this to refuse certain requests. A system that's currently in alarm doesn't get placed on test by a bot — it goes to a person. That's not a gap in the automation, that's the automation doing its job. What I want to show you is what happens on the other side of that decision."

  2. Run the refusal
    ShowCaller identifies the site, asks to place it on test. Eligibility check detects the active alarm. Assistant explains in plain language and transfers with a reason code.
    Say

    "Notice it didn't apologise or fail. It told the caller what was happening and why, then handed off on purpose."

  3. Cut to the desktop
    ShowAgent Workspace with the script already populated: site name and address, system identifier, intent, authentication result, transfer reason. Then transcript, then the auto-generated summary.
    Say

    "The caller hasn't repeated a single thing. The site is on screen, the identification result is on screen, and the reason they're here is on screen. Your operator's first sentence can be useful instead of 'can I take your central station number.'"

    "And notice what isn't on screen. The personal identification code isn't there, and it isn't in the recording or the transcript either. Mario asked us to treat it like the last four of a social security number, so we secured the capture. What's carried forward is whether it validated and whether that person was authorised — not the code itself."

  4. Copilot on the specific exception
    ShowGenesys Agent Copilot surfacing the next step for an in-alarm site, drawn from the same knowledge content used in Scene C.
    Say

    "This is the same knowledge article the self-service side uses. One place to maintain it, two places it shows up. When you change the guidance for an in-alarm system, it changes for the bot and for the operator at the same time."

Talk track — the supervisor half

  1. Show the whole journey on one record
    ShowThe interaction detail: arrival, language selection, intent, identification, eligibility decision, transfer reason, agent handling, wrap-up. One record.
    Say

    "That's one interaction, start to finish. Self-service and the operator on the same record, with the same identifiers."

    "Here's the question I'd ask about your current setup, and I'm genuinely asking. Can you produce one report today that follows a Test-On caller from arrival through to resolution?"

    [Wait. The answer is almost certainly no, and it is better coming from them.]

  2. The two containment numbers — do this before testing scores anything
    ShowFlow Outcome performance for System Test completion; the five instrumented outcomes; wrap code reconciliation.
    Say

    "I want to be straight with you about containment, because it's one of your evaluation criteria and a single number would mislead you."

    "In-scope containment is: of the callers who land in what we automated — standalone fire or burglar, single primary, full test, valid code, duration inside the state maximum — how many finished without a person. That measures what we built."

    "All-traffic containment is: of every Test-On caller, how many finished without a person. That will be lower, and it should be, because combo systems, partial tests and test-off all go to an operator by agreement. Those aren't failures. They're the roadmap."

    "Which brings me to a question I need from you: what share of your monitored base is combo? Because until I know that, neither of us can tell whether the number you're about to see is representative or just a reflection of what we scoped."

  3. Hand the operating model over
    ShowPerformance dashboards filtered for queues, flows, and flow outcomes. Drill into detail. Save a view. Schedule an export.
    Say

    "You'll want your own versions of these. Save a view, filter it to your queues, schedule the export. This is yours to configure — it isn't a report we build for you."

Discovery to extract here

  • What share of the monitored base is combo, and what share is HVAC? (Targets M — this is the single most important number outstanding)
  • What is AHT on a Test-On call that reaches an operator today? (Targets M)
  • Who looks at self-service reporting today, how often, and what decision do they make with it? (Targets Champion)

Scene C — Knowledge and the Timed Change: Who Owns the Next Improvement

12 minutes · Miguel, Ulysses, Mario, and whoever approves prompt language today · Deliver in the IT/administrator portion of knowledge transfer

Objective
Extend automation continuously without a vendor engagement for every change
Metric
Elapsed time and cost per content change; answer coverage over time
Capability
Genesys Cloud Knowledge authored and published by JCI, surfaced to both the virtual agent and the agent desktop
Outcome
Coverage grows as an operational activity rather than as a vendor project
This scene doubles as the FAQ recommendation JCI asked for

On 24 August Rich flagged structured FAQ as low-effort if JCI supplies content, and Mario asked Genesys to recommend whether it should be in scope. That recommendation is still owed. Deliver it here, with working evidence attached, rather than as a paragraph in a document.

Talk track

  1. Set up the real question
    Say

    "You asked us to come back on whether FAQ should be in scope. My answer is yes, but not for the reason you'd expect — and I want to show you why rather than assert it."

    "Your intent list has six intents and every one of them routes to a queue. Billing routes to billing. New service routes to the regional team. Local office routes to a person. There's nothing in there that answers a question."

  2. Ask it the obvious way, then three ways nobody authored
    ShowAsk a configured question. It answers. Then ask the same underlying question with different vocabulary, partial phrasing, and as a compound question. It still answers, sourced from the article.
    Say

    "Every one of those phrasings would be a new intent, a new announcement, and a change request in the way this works today. Here it's one article."

  3. Ask something with no answer
    ShowAn adjacent question with no article. Graceful outcome. Then show it captured as a content gap rather than lost.
    Say

    "It didn't guess. And the miss didn't disappear — it's recorded, so you know what to write next."

  4. The timed change — the point of the whole scene
    ShowOpen the knowledge workbench. Author the missing article. Publish. Re-ask. It answers. Put a clock on it.
    Say

    "Ninety seconds. No ticket, no vendor, no release window."

    "And here's the part I'd actually think hardest about. When you sent us the POC document, there was a note in it from your side saying the flow diagrams couldn't be rebuilt without the incumbent's original files. That's not a criticism of anyone — it's just what the current operating model costs you. If changing an answer needs a vendor, coverage stops growing. Twenty percent containment after two years is what that looks like."

    Judgement call on that last line

    It is the sharpest line in the deck and it references their own document. Deliver it flat and without triumph, or not at all. If Mario is in the room, it will land — he wrote the direction to stop reproducing the incumbent design. If the room is junior, drop the second paragraph and keep the ninety seconds.

  5. The recommendation, stated plainly
    Say

    "So the recommendation is: yes to FAQ, and the two places I'd start are billing and new service. Both are pure routing today, both are high volume by your own intent list, and neither needs an integration to answer. Send us the content you already have — even in the form your agents use — and we'll show you coverage before the readout."

    "Governance-wise: versioned, you control who can publish, and every answer traces back to a source article. In a life-safety business you should be able to point at the document behind an answer. You can."

Discovery to extract here

  • Who approves prompt and answer language today, and how long does a change actually take end to end? (Targets M and Paper Process)
  • Where does the FAQ content live now — agent-facing documents, a knowledge base, or in people's heads? (Practical, and it unblocks the recommendation)
  • After handover, who at JCI owns prompts, intents and knowledge? (Targets Champion and Decision Process)

5. Objection Handling

Prepared responses. Short, direct, no defensiveness.

"Why wouldn't we just keep our contact center platform and replace the bot?"

The real objection, and the one the incumbent will make for you. Do not argue against it.

Say

"You could, and we'd support it. Two things to weigh. First, the four capabilities your SOW asks about — self-service, agent handling, outbound, digital — only come from one place if the platform is one place; otherwise outbound stays where it is and the conversational layer can't see it. Second, the report I showed you that follows a caller from arrival to resolution only exists because both halves are on the same record. Split the stack and you're back to reconciling exports. Neither of those makes the decision for you. They should just be in it rather than discovered afterwards."

"Your POC only contained X percent. We already get twenty."

Pre-empt this in Scene B rather than answering it at the readout.

Say

"Those two numbers measure different populations, which is why we asked to define both up front. Your twenty percent is across all Test-On traffic. Ours is across the band we scoped — and we deliberately excluded combo, partial and test-off because the rules weren't documented well enough to automate safely in four weeks. Compare like with like and then look at what the excluded paths would add. That's the production conversation, and it's the one worth having."

"This is life-safety. How do we trust AI not to make something up?"

Expect this from Darrell or from a security reviewer. It is a fair question and the answer is strong.

Say

"The eligibility decision isn't AI. Whether a system can go on test is deterministic logic in Architect evaluating your rules — account scenario, alarm state, system type, duration against the state maximum. Same inputs, same answer, every time, and you can read the logic. The AI is on the surface: understanding what the caller said and collecting the values. Where we do generate language, it's grounded in your published knowledge articles rather than free-form, so every answer traces to a source document you control. And where the rules say stop — active alarm, ineligible configuration — it stops and hands to a person. That's a design decision, not a limitation we're apologising for."

If pressed on governance: retrieval against approved content bounds the model to what JCI has published, and the audit trail from answer to source article is the artifact a compliance reviewer will ask for. Where tool and data boundaries need to be explicit and traceable, the Model Context Protocol gives that structure on an open, inspectable basis rather than a black box. to-verify — confirm current entitlement and configuration before naming specific capabilities to a security reviewer.

"We already have a dialer."

Say

"Understood — so the question isn't whether you have one, it's whether the notification and the inbound journey report against each other. If a campaign reduces inbound Test-On calls and the two live on different platforms, nobody can prove it. That's the whole argument, and it's a small one. I'd rather you had it than not."

"The Spanish was machine-translated."

Say

"It was, and we said so in writing. That's a POC decision, not a production one — Mario reviewed the terminology and we'd expect professional localisation before anything goes live. What the POC proves is that the journey works in a second language with separate queues and separate reporting, and that adding a third costs the content, not another build."

"Four weeks isn't a fair test of any platform."

Say

"Agreed, which is why we asked what acceptable, good and excellent looked like in the time rather than pretending the constraint away. What four weeks does test is how quickly a design gets from a document to something callable, and how much of that you can change yourself afterwards. Those are the two things that determine what happens in year two."

7. Delivery Notes

Practical constraints on actually getting these delivered.

  • Three scenes, three different rooms. Scene A needs operations leadership and the outbound owner — people the SOW author may not be able to convene. If that room cannot be assembled, that is a qualification finding for the AE, not a reason to deliver it to the wrong audience.
  • Scenes B and C ride inside knowledge transfer. Mario asked for IT navigation, customer-facing handling, and supervisor and manager views. Scene B is the supervisor content. Scene C is the IT content. Delivered in that register they cost no extra meeting.
  • Do not demo in the POC tenant. Use an SC-owned org. A build issue must not taint a demo and a demo artifact must not appear in a scored environment.
  • Stub, never rebuild. Scene B needs a transfer point, not a Test-On journey. Anything that looks like a parallel build creates a coordination problem with PS.
  • Pick the exception jointly with the PS lead. Scene B is written around active alarm — unambiguous, urgent, and a correct refusal rather than a failure. Confirm that is the exception PS implemented.
  • Each scene has three discovery questions. They are the reason the scene earns its slot. If you deliver the capability and leave without the answers, the demo was entertainment.
  • Held in reserve: Speech and Text Analytics, workforce engagement, and AI-driven quality management. All real, none with scoring weight, and showing them early tells the incumbent's sponsor exactly what you are after.

Change log (v1 → v4)

  • Reordered the scenes to put outbound first — the dialer question is unanswered on JCI's side and a recommendation is worth more before their internal view forms than after.
  • Replaced asserted value with ranges and marked every figure lacking a JCI baseline as to-verify. The all-traffic containment range now carries an explicit warning that it is unreliable without the combo share of the base.
  • Rewrote the containment passage in Scene B as a pre-emption delivered before testing rather than a defence offered at the readout, and moved the combo-share question into the talk track where it is natural to ask.
  • Recast Scene C from a knowledge demo into the FAQ recommendation JCI actually requested on 24 August, with the demo as supporting evidence and a specific starting recommendation.
  • Added three discovery questions per scene, each targeting a named MEDDPICC gap, and flagged the economic buyer and decision process as AE-blocking rather than background.
  • Tightened the register throughout: Scenes B and C are written to be delivered inside knowledge transfer as operating guidance, not as competitive pitch, and the sharpest line in Scene C now carries an explicit judgement call on when not to use it.
