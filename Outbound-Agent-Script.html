Dialing strategy & scale

Q.Why are you only running Agentless — what about the other dialing modes?

Agentless is the right fit for a notification with no agent judgment required. Preview, Progressive, Power, and Predictive all exist and are documented — they trade agent control for dialing efficiency in different ways. The recommendation in Beat 6 covers when each one earns its place; nothing here rules them out for a different use case.

Q.Could we run Predictive with our team to see it live?

Genesys's own guidance recommends at least fifteen agents for a meaningful Predictive sample — a small pool doesn't produce an adequate basis for the agent-availability calculation. Progressive is the honest live-proof mode for a POC; Predictive is worth a configuration and architecture conversation once you know your real agent population.

Q.Can agents handle inbound and outbound in the same session?

Yes, blended operation is supported. The one design consideration is that inbound work can change an agent's availability between when a call is dialed and when it connects — Progressive's precise/reservation option reduces that risk if you build an agent-based use case later.

Q.What actually limits throughput if this scales up?

Not just the dialer algorithm. Agent population (for agent-based modes), eligible contact population, your org's calls-per-second limit (15/sec by default, raisable through a Genesys Care request), telephony capacity, line allocation, contact rates, call duration, and compliance settings all contribute.

Compliance & controls

Q.Does Genesys guarantee TCPA or other regulatory compliance?

No — and don't let that be the answer either way. Genesys supplies policy-enforcement controls: DNC, attempt restrictions, contactable time windows, abandonment thresholds. Your legal and compliance function defines the policy those controls implement.

Q.What stops you from calling someone too often?

Attempt Controls, if configured, cap dials per contact or per number by day or in total. Important nuance: if no attempt-control entry is assigned, no limit is imposed at all — so this has to be explicit in any production design, not assumed.

Q.How is local calling time enforced across your seven time zones?

Contactable Time Sets, or Automatic Time Zone Mapping for supported North American data. Worth being precise about one distinction: campaign schedule controls whether the campaign is running at all; contactable time controls whether an individual record is eligible right now. A campaign can be running while a specific record stays temporarily ineligible — that's not a bug, that's the two controls doing different jobs.

Q.What's the difference between a true abandon and a compliance abandon?

True Abandon: the customer disconnected before the call ever reached an agent or flow. Compliance Abandon: the customer answered but wasn't routed within the Compliance Abandon Threshold — two seconds by default. A call can be a Compliance Abandon without ever being a True Abandon; they measure different things.

Data, reporting & reconciliation

Q.How do you know a message was actually delivered versus left on a machine?

That's exactly what Beat 5 shows — call analysis distinguishes Live Voice from Answering Machine, and the response set sends each to an independent flow with its own wrap-up. It's not inferred after the fact; it's a real-time classification driving a different path.

Q.Can we reconcile Genesys's numbers against our own systems or CRM?

Yes. A stable source identifier — Contact_ID in this build — plus Contact List, Campaign, Conversation ID, timestamps, and exported attempt/interaction detail gives you the correlation model: source record ↔ campaign ↔ attempt ↔ conversation ↔ disposition.

Q.Is reporting limited to this UI?

No. Performance views handle operational analysis, saved views give supervisors a repeatable perspective, exports and scheduled exports produce files for reconciliation, Analytics APIs support custom BI or a data lake, and Outbound APIs allow programmatic management of the outbound objects themselves. One caution: don't claim every UI metric maps one-to-one to a single API field — for production design, that gets validated against the specific endpoint in the Developer Center.

Q.What does "Contact" actually mean in this reporting?

A connected call — it does not prove the intended person was reached. That's Right Party Contact, which further classifies as Success, Neutral, or Failure. Dials, Contacts, and RPC are three different counts; conflating them is the most common misread of outbound reporting.

Competitive & positioning

Q.Doesn't NICE already have an outbound dialer? What's actually different here?

NICE has a dialer — this isn't a capability argument. The difference is that the conversational layer, routing, agent desktop, analytics, and dialer all sit on one platform with one contact model. In a stack where the AI or self-service vendor is separate from the dialer, that vendor cannot see or influence outbound at all. Beat 7's reporting comparison is the concrete version of this point — same contacts, same platform, one place to reconcile.

Q.Why doesn't the notification flow ask for or verify a PIC?

Deliberate, and consistent with the inbound design: the notification confirms a site and offers an action, and never states or requests a PIC. An authorization decision doesn't happen over an unauthenticated outbound call in either direction of this build.
